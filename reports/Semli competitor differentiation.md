# Semli wins on the combination, not the AI

Semli is not differentiated by AI import. As of October 2026, at least a dozen dedicated iOS and web tools turn a syllabus PDF or photo into dated deadlines. Several of them show a review step before saving and charge less than Semli. Apple, Google, Todoist and every major chatbot now turn a screenshot into a calendar event for free. The internal line "nobody in this tier does AI import from screenshots and syllabi" (`docs/pricing-and-positioning.md`, Oct 19) is false and should be deleted everywhere it has spread. What the notes did not find anywhere is the combination Semli actually ships: classes, irregular work shifts and deadlines in one plan; overlap warnings across all three; a per-item evidence line with whole-import undo; and, in Plus, a diff of a new shift screenshot against shifts already saved. Two of those pieces are close to unique. Diffing a re-imported shift schedule appeared in no product found, and no consumer app positions itself on "working student." Neither has direct evidence of student demand. The differentiators with the strongest demand evidence are the review/provenance step and the "basics stay free" promise, and both are trust and pricing stances rather than features a competitor cannot copy. My mentor's read: position Semli as the planner for students who work, lead with "check every date before it's saved," and stop selling extraction. Then validate the shift wedge fast, because no other differentiator is both rare and defensible. A caveat on all of this: the evidence is mostly search snippets and competitor-run blogs, and the App Store, Reddit and several vendor domains were blocked during research.

## How much weight the evidence can bear

Read the rest of this report with the following limits in mind. **No App Store listing was opened directly.** apps.apple.com, reddit.com, dormway.app, mystudylife.com, coursicle.com, 9to5mac.com, macrumors.com, nces.ed.gov and several shift-app sites were blocked in the research sessions. So every competitor feature, price and rating here comes from a search-engine summary of the page, a third-party mirror (AppBrain, AppFollow) or a review blog. Many of the "best syllabus app 2026" comparisons are written by competitors: DormWay, Coursicle, PassAI, IntelliPlan, SyllabusAI, MyStudyLife, Power Planner, Carly, Morgen and Lifestack. Several sources contradict each other on price. ClassPulse's App Store listing and its own site disagree about the free tier ([classpulse.store](https://www.classpulse.store/)), and Shovel is quoted at both $9.79/mo and roughly $20/mo ([Coursicle](https://www.coursicle.com/blog/shovel-vs-coursicle/); [Mindomax](https://www.mindomax.com/best-study-planner-apps)). **There are no verbatim Reddit quotes** from general student subreddits. The only forum evidence is r/StudentNurse threads read through a mirror and allnurses posts. "Nobody does X" findings rest on absence of evidence within a limited search budget, which is weaker than evidence of absence. That applies above all to shift-schedule diffing and to ringing alarms. iOS 27 / WWDC 2026 announcements were not researched, and they are the single largest unknown threat.

In short, the commoditization findings are well-supported, because many independent listings say the same thing. The uniqueness findings are provisional. The demand findings are strongest where academic surveys exist (AI trust) and weakest exactly where Semli is most distinctive (shifts).

## The "nobody does AI import" claim collapsed in 2026

The notes list a crowded field of dedicated syllabus scanners, many with App IDs in the 675–680 million range, which suggests launches between late 2025 and 2026 (inferred from the ID ranges only):

- **Syllabot** accepts a PDF, Word doc, photo or link and lets the user **review deadlines before saving** to the calendar ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/syllabot/id6758374658)).
- **SyllaScan** lets users edit dates before adding them ([App Store MY](https://apps.apple.com/my/app/syllascan/id6756897540)).
- **ClassPulse** shows extracted rows for review before anything saves, and charges **$5.99 per semester or $17.99 a year** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/classpulse-gpa-study-plan/id6805395851)).
- **Syllabus AI** Pro is **$12.99 a year** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/syllabus-ai/id6755570335)).
- **SyllabAI** on the web is free, with unlimited extraction at **$3.99/mo** ([usesyllabai.com](https://usesyllabai.com/)).
- Sylly, Semora and SyllySync fill out the single-purpose tier.

Full planners have the feature too. **DormWay** reads assignments, exams and grading weights from syllabi, syncs Canvas, Blackboard and Moodle, and is free. Its blog even recommends a "source-backed" parser that links each event to the syllabus page, which is the closest thing to Semli's evidence line found anywhere ([DormWay](https://dormway.app/blog/ai-syllabus-parser-automating-your-academic-schedule-in-2026)). **Shovel** builds a semester plan from a syllabus PDF or from LMS sync with Canvas, Brightspace, Moodle and Google Classroom ([Coursicle](https://www.coursicle.com/blog/shovel-vs-coursicle/)). **MyStudyLife**, the largest dedicated planner (about 4.5 stars from about 6.2K US ratings), says on its own tour page that its "Scout" assistant builds a schedule from a timetable photo, a screenshot or a syllabus ([MyStudyLife tour](https://mystudylife.com/tour/); [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/appstore/my-study-life-school-planner/ios-910639339)). Competitors dispute the syllabus part, so it needs a hands-on test.

The same thing happened on the work side. At least four iOS shift apps advertise AI photo or screenshot import of a work schedule: Shift2Cal, The Shift, Work Log and **ShiftKal**. ShiftKal's listing reads "Import a schedule from a photo or screenshot, then review it before saving" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/shiftkal-shift-work-calendar/id6784589897)). Employer tools are also adding AI schedule importers: 7shifts has one for managers ([7shifts KB](https://kb.7shifts.com/hc/en-us/articles/50726979562003)).

## Apple, Google and the chatbots now set the free floor

The real baseline is not another planner. It is a free assistant the student already has.

- **Apple, iOS 26.** On an iPhone 15 Pro or later, a screenshot with event details shows "Add to Calendar", which opens an editable preview ([gHacks, Mar 2026](https://www.ghacks.net/2026/03/06/ios-26-lets-you-instantly-add-calendar-events-from-screenshots/)).
- **Google.** Gemini takes a schedule photo, previews the events and writes them to Google Calendar after you confirm, though reportedly only to the default calendar ([photo2calendar](https://photo2calendar.com/how-to-use-google-gemini-add-events-from-photo)). Google is giving eligible US college students **a free year of Google AI Pro if they sign up by Dec 31, 2026**, renewing at $19.99/mo after that ([MacRumors, snippet](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/19/students-free-year-google-ai-pro/)).
- **Anthropic.** Claude on iOS creates calendar events and recurring reminders on every plan ([Anthropic support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11869619-use-claude-with-ios-apps)).
- **Universities teach the workaround.** Farmingdale State tells students to have ChatGPT turn a syllabus into a Google Calendar CSV, then to "verify dates/times against the official syllabus or Brightspace before importing" ([Farmingdale](https://www.farmingdale.edu/news/ram-program/2025/2025-09-04-build-your-semester-calendar-with-chatgpt-google-calendar.shtml)).
- **Free .ics converters** cover the rest: text-2-ics, text-to-cal, Taskade, an open-source converter that shows a review beside the source PDF, and others ([GitHub](https://github.com/520HXC/Syllabus-Converter-)).
- **Possibly iOS 27.** One shift-app vendor claims Apple Calendar "may" handle photo imports on iOS 27 ([Lumical, snippet](https://lumical.app/blog/shift-calendar-apps)). That claim is unverified, and WWDC 2026 was not checked.

None of these platform tools was documented doing the rest of Semli's job. None reconciles a whole semester of recurring classes with irregular shifts, flags conflicts across them, links each date to where it was read, or escalates from a reminder to an alarm. The general-AI research put it this way: chatbots create events but don't own the plan afterward. That gap is real, but it is a gap in *ongoing management*, not in *reading*. The implication is uncomfortable for the current copy. Semli's store one-liner, "The daily planner that reads your syllabus so you don't have to," and the strategy doc's "make importing the signature feature" sell exactly the part Apple and Google give away. The defensible pitch is about what happens after the read: the date is checked, it sits next to your shifts, and Semli tells you when they collide.

The Canvas Student app is a second, quieter floor. It now has a cross-course To Do list, a widget and, since May 2026, a "Centralized Coursework" dashboard. It shows only Canvas items that instructors give due dates, and it cannot hold shifts or a second LMS ([Rutgers](https://canvas.rutgers.edu/2026/05/25/canvas-student-dashboard-changes/); [Instructure](https://community.instructure.com/en/kb/articles/662709-viewing-my-personal-to-do-items-in-the-canvas-student-app)). For a student with Canvas-only courses and no job, Canvas Student covers most of "what's due" for free. Semli's argument to that student is thin.

The tier list gives the honest answer to the founder's question. **Every individual Semli feature exists somewhere. What doesn't exist is the bundle aimed at one person: the student who works hourly shifts.** That is a legitimate differentiator; most good products are differentiated by combination. But it is easier to copy than the founder may hope. MyStudyLife already detects conflicts and is moving to paid AI tiers. DormWay already treats clinicals as shifts. Any syllabus scanner could add a "work" event type in a sprint. The shift-diff is the one piece with real engineering depth (matching, moved/added/dropped detection, partial acceptance). It is also the piece aimed at the narrowest situation: a student re-importing a changing schedule.

Two weak spots deserve candour. First, LMS sync. Semli uses read-only iCal feeds, and Shovel and Coursicle pull directly from four to six LMSs. Coursicle and DormWay give LMS sync away free, while Semli puts Canvas resync behind Plus (`pricing-and-positioning.md`, access audit). That paywall is out of step with a market where ongoing LMS sync is free. Second, employer feeds. When I Work, 7shifts, Sling (paid plans) and UKG/Kronos all publish one-way iCal shift feeds ([When I Work](https://help.wheniwork.com/articles/syncing-your-schedule-to-a-calendar-app-computer); [7shifts](https://kb.7shifts.com/hc/en-us/articles/4861793532691); [UKG](https://hlp02.gss.mykronos.com/help/en_US/Content/ApplicationSetup/EmployeeSelfService/ConfigureCalendarSynchronization.htm)). For a student whose job uses one of those tools, a live feed beats re-screenshotting every week, so Semli's diff is solving a problem the feed already solves. The screenshot wedge is strongest where no export exists: Target myTime, where workers install Chrome extensions to get shifts out ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/mytime-to-calendar/odekbnpmjmbggpaeglaljcgapeoknhcp)), Me@Walmart, Starbucks, paper rotas and clinical rosters.

## Demand evidence is strongest where Semli is least unique

The evidence for each differentiator runs almost exactly opposite to its rarity.

The **review and provenance step** has the best support:

- In the CSU system survey, **88% of 94,060 students** said verifying AI output is necessary ([CSU](https://www.calstate.edu/impact-of-the-csu/technology/ai-empowered-csu/Documents/csu-ahead-of-the-curve-summary.pdf?v=2)).
- At Duke, **90% of students want AI to be transparent about its limitations** ([Duke CTL](https://ctl.duke.edu/caradite/ai-student-survey/ai-accuracy-and-limitations/)).
- An AERA 2026 paper found **40% of 1,289 students** were "uncritical" (high trust, low verification) ([AERA](https://convention2.allacademic.com/one/aera/aera26/online_program_direct_link/view_paper/2272031/)). For the review step, this means it has to make checking effortless rather than rely on diligence.
- A Structured reviewer complained there was "no option to edit what AI came up with without first adding the events to your day" ([App Store, snippet](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/structured-daily-planner-todo/id1499198946?see-all=reviews)).
- Farmingdale's "Brightspace wins" guidance shows institutions already frame AI dates as needing a check.

This is real demand, but it is demand for a trait that is becoming table stakes. Semli's edge is the *quality* of the check (the evidence line, flagged TBAs, no invented times, undo), not its existence.

**Free basics** have the second-strongest support. Most of the recent anger at MyStudyLife is about paywall creep and "an expensive subscription fee" ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/my-study-life-school-planner/com.virblue.mystudylife)). Tiimo draws "predatory" trial complaints ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tiimo.androidappreactnative&hl=en_US)). Shovel's price is "the main complaint in reviews" ([AppFollow](https://appfollow.io/ios/shovel-study-planner/1467742357?country=us)). A Polish university study found students "would rather not use the application than pay for it" (directional only, not US) ([Lupa & Wójcik](https://managementpapers.polsl.pl/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/194-Lupa-W%C3%B3jcik.pdf)). The promise is a good wedge against the incumbents, but it is a stance, and Power Planner, Coursicle and DormWay are already free.

**Shifts, classes and deadlines in one plan** is plausible but unvalidated:

- **The population is large.** NCES counted 40% of full-time and 74% of part-time undergraduates employed in 2020, and Georgetown CEW (2015, stale) put about 40% working 30+ hours a week ([NCES, snippet](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/indicator/ssa/college-student-employment/); [CEW](https://cew.georgetown.edu/workinglearnerspressrelease)).
- **The pain shows up only in nursing.** Forum evidence of friction comes from nursing students, whose clinicals are released "at the last possible second" ([allnurses](https://allnurses.com/working-nursing-school-t472710)).
- **No one asks for this app.** No thread was found asking for an app that merges shifts and classes. Students cope with Google Calendar, colour-coding and paper. UMass's advice to put "classes, extracurriculars, and job schedules" in one calendar shows the job-to-be-done exists ([UMass](https://websites.umass.edu/askcomm/category/clubs-and-organizations)), but not a willingness to pay for it.

The **shift diff**, **ringing alarms** and **suggested rescheduling** have no demand evidence in the notes at all, positive or negative. **Local-first privacy** never surfaced as a student concern; the April–May 2026 Canvas breach is a talking point, not a demonstrated driver ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Canvas_data_breach)). **iPhone-only** is cheap at launch given 87% teen iPhone ownership ([Piper Sandler](https://www.pipersandler.com/news/piper-sandler-completes-50th-semi-annual-teen-survey)). The bigger gap is laptop setup, since Semli's own survey found 65% do schoolwork mainly on a laptop.

|---|---|---|
| Review step with evidence line and undo | Review common; evidence plus undo rare | Strong (CSU, Duke, AERA, reviewer complaints) |
| Free basics | Common among rivals | Strong (paywall backlash at MyStudyLife, Tiimo, Shovel) |
| Classes, shifts and deadlines with overlap warnings | Rare as a bundle | Plausible, unvalidated; population large, pull unproven |

## Semli's price is mid-market against a $0 ceiling

At $24.99 a year and $14.99 for six months (student), Semli sits between two clusters. It is **below** the established planners: MyStudyLife+ at $29.99–39.99, Shovel at about $35, Coursicle's $29.99 tier, TickTick at about $36, Todoist Pro at about $60, Tiimo at $54–80, and the auto-schedulers at $200–400 a year ([Morgen](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/todoist-pricing); [Lifestack](https://lifestack.ai/blog/tiimo-pricing)). It is **above** the new AI-native syllabus tools: ClassPulse at $17.99/yr or **$5.99 per semester**, Syllabus AI at $12.99/yr and SyllabAI at $3.99/mo. It is far above the legacy one-time options, Power Planner's $4.99 lifetime and myHomework's roughly $4.99/yr. Structured, the look-alike the team tracks most closely, is quoted at $19.99–29.99/yr and $64.99–99.99 lifetime depending on source and region ([toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/structured); [habitbox](https://habitbox.app/blog/structured-app-review)).

Two pricing facts matter more than the band. First, **ClassPulse charges $5.99 per semester for AI import against Semli's $14.99 per six months**. If Semli's paid value were import, it would be priced at 2.5 times a direct rival. The price only holds if Plus is understood as the working-student layer: shift diff, suggested times, alarms and LMS resync. Second, the true ceiling is $0. Gemini comes free with Google AI Pro for students who sign up by Dec 31, Claude's calendar actions work on every plan, and Apple Intelligence is built in. The renewal cliff for Google's free year lands across 2027, which the general-planner notes flag as a speculative re-engagement moment. The $4.99 monthly price falls inside the "$1–5" band where 47% of Semli's own (small, single-network) survey placed a reasonable price, and the yearly plan at $2.08/mo sits comfortably in it. Lifetime pricing is praised in reviews as "rare user-friendliness" ([toolguide](https://toolguide.io/en/tool/structured/)), and Semli doesn't offer it. That is worth testing against the semester plan.

## Where the Oct 19 strategy docs are now out of date

Several lines in `docs/pricing-and-positioning.md` and `docs/strategy.md` no longer survive the evidence:

- **"Nobody in this tier does AI import from screenshots and syllabi. That is the thing to charge for."** This is false on both counts. Import is everywhere, and it is the worst thing to charge for, because it is the one feature with a free substitute.
- **"Structured: the closest look-alike; no AI import."** It sits awkwardly beside `strategy.md`'s own note that "Structured already has AI planning and scanning." The notes found AI task creation in Structured but no photo import. Meanwhile **Tiimo ships "snap to plan"** and **Todoist ships Task Capture**, so the closest AI-import look-alikes are not Structured.
- **"The student planner market has settled at $30–40 per year."** The AI-native entrants have opened a $13–18 tier below it.
- **"Make importing the signature feature"** and the store one-liner about reading your syllabus. Both point the brand at the commoditized layer.
- **"Canvas sync as the first paid-only feature."** Free LMS sync at Coursicle and DormWay makes resync a weak paywall.
- **"Win nursing first."** Still sound, but DormWay and Fhynix now market to nursing students specifically, so it is no longer an empty niche.
- `strategy.md`'s revenue table still uses the old **$29.99** yearly price, while the pricing doc moved to **$24.99** on Oct 21. The payer counts need recomputing (an all-yearly mix now needs about 9,600 payers for $20K MRR, not 8,000).
- The team's review mining found MyStudyLife's **recent** rating at 1.62, while AppBrain shows **about 4.5 all-time**. Both can be true, but the "incumbent is failing" argument should cite the recent-review window explicitly.

What still holds: never metering the basics, the trial-reminder rule, the review step as the trust story, and the instinct to target working and nursing students.

## Positioning recommendations

**Rename the category Semli competes in.** "Daily planner for college students" puts Semli in a field of 234+ shift apps, a dozen syllabus scanners and every free calendar ([Appshunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/topics/shift-calendar-work)). "The planner for students who work" is unclaimed. It describes the one bundle nobody else ships, and it addresses roughly half of undergraduates. Keep "student planner" in the App Store keywords for search volume, but make the headline about school and work. Example: "Classes, shifts and deadlines in one week. Semli tells you when they collide."

**Sell the check, not the read.** Lead the import story with the evidence line and nothing-saved-until-you-confirm ("every date shows where it came from"). Don't lead with "AI reads your syllabus." Build the review for the 40% who won't check: highlight low-confidence rows, flag TBAs and never invent times. That turns a common feature into a visibly better one.

**Make the shift diff the Plus centrepiece, and prove it first.** It is the only feature with no competitor found. Before investing more in it, run the validation the notes call for: 15–20 interviews with students in retail, food service and healthcare who re-screenshot schedules weekly, and hand-check r/Target, r/starbucksbaristas and r/StudentNurse threads about getting shifts into a calendar. If students mostly have iCal feeds (When I Work, 7shifts, UKG), add **employer iCal feed subscription** and keep screenshots for myTime, Walmart, Starbucks and paper rosters. Otherwise a feed-using rival will look more convenient.

**Fix the price story to match the value.** Plus should be described as the working-student layer (diff, suggested times, alarms, resync), never as "unlimited imports," because ClassPulse undercuts that at $5.99 a semester. Consider making Canvas/Brightspace resync free, to neutralize Coursicle and DormWay, and metering the shift-specific features instead. Test a lifetime or one-time "year of school" option against the semester plan.

**Treat Apple as a deadline.** Check WWDC 2026 and the iOS 27 betas now for multi-event or photo-to-Calendar import, and for AlarmKit adoption by rivals. If Apple ships batch import, the remaining moat is cross-type conflicts, the shift diff and the plan itself, so build those to depth.

**Close the evidence gaps that block decisions.** Open the App Store listings by hand to confirm ratings and prices for ClassPulse, DormWay, Shovel, ShiftKal and Syllabot. Hands-on test MyStudyLife's Scout syllabus claim and Coursicle's disputed upload. Search directly for any shift app that diffs schedules.

## Conclusion

The founder's instinct that AI import was the moat was correct about twelve months too late. In 2026, reading a syllabus became a feature of the operating system, so the scarce thing is now trust in the dates and context around them. Semli has a credible answer to both: an evidence line nobody else shows, and a plan that knows about your 4-to-10 shift. But it is currently marketing the commodity and pricing as if the commodity were the product. The sharper position is narrower and rests on less evidence: the working student, with the shift diff as its proof.

That trade-off is the real strategic decision. Semli can stay broad, keep "student planner" as the label, and compete on trust and free basics against a dozen look-alikes and free assistants. Or it can bet on a niche with a large population but unproven pull, and make that bet cheap to test before spring registration closes. The data here argues for the second option, run as an experiment. The first option has no differentiator that survives contact with ClassPulse, DormWay and iOS 26.
