# Demand side: US parents and family logistics (school/activity info into a shared calendar)

Research date: Oct 30, 2026. Network note: bostonglobe.com, yahoo.com, edsby.com and 36kr.com could not be fetched in this session (host not allowed), so several findings below come from search-result snippets rather than full-page reads. Stats sourced from vendors that sell school-communication or family-organizer products are flagged as such.

## 1. Volume of school/activity communication and the "mental load"

### Takeaway
The pain is well documented and is getting more press attention in 2026 ("app overload", "platformization of school"). Parents report about 20 school/extracurricular emails a week and 10 to 15 apps. Mothers carry most of the calendar work: 83% of US mothers say keeping the family calendar is "mostly me".

### Cited Findings
- Yahoo/Censuswide survey: parents receive about 20 school and extracurricular emails a week (about 4 a day). Year is not confirmed. The article was about a year old at search time, so likely late 2025. — [Yahoo: "Parents signed up for classroom updates. They got digital burnout instead."](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/family-relationships/article/parents-signed-up-for-classroom-updates-they-got-digital-burnout-instead-090044802.html) (snippet only)
- Cornerstone/Edsby study: 85% of parents rated their satisfaction with having multiple school apps at 5/10 or lower, and some parents juggle 10 to 15 apps at a time. Vendor source (Edsby sells a consolidated platform), and the sample and country were not verifiable. — [Edsby](https://www.edsby.com/school-apps-for-parent-engagement-k12-data/commentary/) (snippet only)
- 54% of K-12 schools reportedly use 10 to 15 distinct educational apps. Vendor blog, and it partly re-cites Edsby. — [BeeNet: The 10-App Problem](https://beenet.app/blog/the-10-app-problem-why-fragmented-school-communication-fails/)
- Renton & McCrindle (2026): 26% of parents name "too many apps and platforms" as a problem, 24% get too much irrelevant information, and 19% struggle to know where to find what they need. McCrindle is an Australian research firm, so this is likely not US data. — [The Edu Flaneuse, Aug 2026](https://theeduflaneuse.com/2026/08/23/the-cognitive-load-of-school-communication/)
- Boston Globe (Oct 5, 2026) headline: "Two kids, 12 apps, and zero clue? Welcome to the platformization of school, sports, and life, parents." This is mainstream press framing of the exact problem as of this month. — [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/05/lifestyle/parents-schools-edtech-apps-platforms-passwords/) (could not fetch body)
- Districts are responding by consolidating into single district-managed platforms. This supply-side trend could reduce the pain from inside schools, but it does nothing for sports or activities. — [The Learning Standard](https://thelearningstandard.org/news/how-schools-are-tackling-parent-app-fatigue-and-tech-overload); [The Learning Standard: consolidation](https://thelearningstandard.org/news/why-the-parent-teacher-app-boom-is-forcing-schools-to-consolidate)
- Weeks et al., Journal of Marriage and Family (2025), survey of about 3,000 US parents. Mothers report handling about 71% of household mental-load tasks (fathers about 45%; these are self-reports, so they sum above 100%). Mothers report 79% of daily tasks. **83% of mothers say they are "mostly me" for keeping track of the family calendar** (e.g., medical appointments), one of the highest items. Fathers overestimate their share. — [Wiley / JMF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jomf.13057); [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/moms-mental-load-28244/)
- Youth sports time load: the average sports parent spends about 3 hours 23 minutes on each day their child has a practice or game. 62% of sports parents volunteer, averaging more than 4 hours a week (Aspen Project Play, n=1,848; outlets disagree on whether this is the 2024 or 2025 survey). — [Project Play](https://projectplay.org/news/project-play-survey-parents-spend-3-hours-on-sports-every-day-their-child-plays)

### Inferences
- The buyer is overwhelmingly the mother (the "default parent"). Positioning and channels should target her, and the "shared" part of the calendar exists mainly to hand off load to the other parent.
- School consolidation could shrink the "number of apps" pain over time. Sports and activity scheduling (TeamSnap, GameChanger, club emails) stays fragmented, which argues for leading with activities plus school rather than school alone.

### Gaps
- No independent US survey with a stated sample counting school apps per parent. ParentSquare, Remind and Bloomz usage stats were not retrieved, and no "minutes per week spent on family calendar" figure was found.

## 2. Missed events, forms and deadlines

### Takeaway
There is clear anecdotal and press evidence that parents miss things, but citable survey numbers are weak and mostly come from vendors.

### Cited Findings
- "60% of parents surveyed miss important events or details in their inbox." Vendor blog with an unclear primary source. — [BeeNet](https://beenet.app/blog/what-parents-actually-want-from-school-technology-and-the-gap/)
- A productivity blog attributes "62% of parents have missed school information buried in email" (and "71% of those felt like bad parents") to a 2024 Censuswide/Yahoo survey. I could not locate the original, so treat it as unverified. — [Nestify blog](https://www.nestifyapp.org/blog/stop-forgetting-school-deadlines-permission-slips)
- Older research on consent forms: parents who did not return forms usually had not seen them or lacked time, rather than objecting. Few parents recalled receiving the form, yet they over-reported returning it. — [Clinician.com](https://www.clinician.com/articles/17481-active-or-passive-gaining-consent-from-parents-for-student-surveys) (older; date not shown)
- A parent quoted on juggling several kids' sports: "The only time it gets frustrating is when something changes at the very last minute." Families cope with a shared iPhone calendar or an organizer app. — [MHSAA: Parents Master Art of Schedule Juggling](https://www.mhsaa.com/topics/viewpoints/parents-master-art-schedule-juggling?page=12)

### Inferences
- "Updates when schedules change" is the most emotionally loaded feature, because last-minute changes are the named frustration. It is also hardest to deliver, since it requires ongoing ingestion (email forwarding or inbox access), not one-off screenshots.

### Gaps
- No rigorous US survey on how often parents miss events or forms. The original Yahoo/Censuswide release should be checked (yahoo.com was blocked here).

## 3. Willingness to pay: paid family organizers

### Takeaway
Parents do pay for this exact job. Skylight charges $79/yr for Calendar Plus, which gates AI email/PDF/photo-to-calendar, on top of $300 to $600 hardware. Cozi charges $39/yr (Gold) and $79/yr (Max, with AI). Co-parenting apps charge $77 to $350+ per parent per year. AI-native entrants (Ohai, Milo) price at $10 to $40 a month. Hard data on subscriber counts and conversion is scarce and mostly stale.

### Cited Findings
- **Skylight Calendar Plus: $79/year, annual only.** It gates "Magic Import" (email/PDF to calendar) and "Sidekick" (photo scanning, e.g., school newsletters and flyers). Sources differ on which name covers which feature. The free tier keeps Google/iCal/Outlook sync, events, chores and lists. Hardware is about $299.99 (15" Calendar 2) to $599.99 (27" Calendar Max), verified Aug 2026 by a third party. — [Skylight support](https://skylight.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/35984779668379-What-does-a-Skylight-Calendar-cost); [getsense.ai (competitor)](https://getsense.ai/blog/posts/skylight-calendar-hidden-costs-paywall); [Taste of Home review](https://www.tasteofhome.com/article/skylight-calendar-review/)
- Owner communities complain about the subscription "doubling" from $39 to $79. Some sources say the $39 was actually Frame Plus, a different product, so this is a perception issue. — [getsense.ai](https://getsense.ai/blog/posts/skylight-calendar-hidden-costs-paywall.html)
- Skylight scale (2023, stale): over 5M total users, nearly 1.5M units sold, about 300K subscribers. It raised $15M in credit from SG Credit Partners. — [BusinessWire, Aug 2023](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230801733428/en/Skylight-the-Leading-Digital-Photo-Frame-and-Smart-Calendar-Brand-Secures-$15M-from-SG-Credit-Partners-to-Continue-to-Deliver-on-its-Mission-of-Connecting-Loved-Ones)
- Skylight revenue estimates conflict widely ($10M to $50M a year; one tracker estimates about $31M in 2024 online sales). Not reliable. — [Grips Intelligence](https://gripsintelligence.com/insights/retailers/skylightframe.com); [Growjo](https://growjo.com/company/skylightframe.com)
- **Cozi Gold: $39/year** (web or in-app). One subscription covers the whole family account and removes ads. **Cozi Max: $79/year** with AI tools. — [Cozi Gold page](https://www.cozi.com/cozi-gold/); [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/best/calendar-apps/cozi/)
- Cozi says it has "20M+ registered members". The figure is undated and likely cumulative or stale. 1M members dates from about 2010. — [Xconomy (old)](https://legacy.xconomy.com/?p=6506); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cozi)
- A 2026 Chinese-language report (36Kr) reportedly says Gold accounts for a much larger share of Cozi subscriptions than Max: users pay for core organizing rather than AI add-ons. Snippet only; I could not fetch it. — [36Kr](https://www.36kr.com/p/3896941067716481)
- OurFamilyWizard (co-parenting) bought Cozi from Dotdash Meredith in May 2022. The parent company is now "In Tandem" (OurFamilyWizard, Cozi, FamilyWall, Custody Navigator). Cozi had 11 employees at the time. — [PR Newswire, 2022](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/ourfamilywizard-acquires-cozi-1-family-organizing-app-301539136.html); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2022/seattle-family-organization-app-cozi-acquired-by-co-parenting-app-ourfamilywizard/); [Newswire: In Tandem](https://www.newswire.com/news/ourfamilywizard-launches-new-parent-company-in-tandem-22427670)
- **Ohai.ai** (AI household assistant for "Chief Household Officers") raised a $6M seed co-led by Eniac Ventures and LifeX (Dec 2023), plus an undisclosed early-stage deal in Aug 2025. The App Store reportedly lists Premium at $14.99, Premium Plus at $24.99 and Premium Group at $39.99 (checked Oct 2026 by a competitor review site); older sources say from $9.99/mo. — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ohaiai-launches-ai-virtual-assistant-to-revolutionize-home-and-personal-organization-in-support-of-chief-household-officers-302040138.html); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/553181-59); [RemindHer review (competitor)](https://remindher.app/ohai-review/)
- **Milo** (AI family scheduling) is reportedly $14.99/mo after a trial. CB Insights shows a "Milo" family-collaboration company with $3.55M over 3 rounds (last seed $1.17M, May 2023). It may not be the same entity. — [RemindHer roundup](https://remindher.app/ai-family-assistant-app/); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/milo-4/financials)
- A crowded AI-family-assistant field exists in 2026 (RemindHer, Klonr, Maple, Sense, Hearth-type displays). Several publish "best AI family assistant" comparison pages, a sign of active SEO competition. — [RemindHer](https://remindher.app/ai-family-assistant-app/); [Klonr](https://klonr.app/blog/best-ai-assistant-busy-parents-2026); [Maple vs Skylight](https://www.growmaple.com/blog-posts/maple-vs-skylight)
- Subscription benchmarks (RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026, 115K apps, $16B+ revenue): first-year retention of annual subscribers is 28% for freemium vs 27% for hard paywall. AI apps show about 41% higher year-1 realized LTV but churn about 30% faster. Productivity has the highest "delayed trial" rate (22%). Trials of 17+ days convert about 70% better than trials of 3 days or less. Annual reactivation is about 5%. I found no "family"-specific category cut. — [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/05/27/new-report-shows-annual-app-subscribers-rarely-return-after-they-cancel/); [SaaStr summary](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth)

### Inferences
- The price anchor for software-only "family calendar + AI import" is about $39 to $79 a year per household. Skylight proves families will pay $79/yr specifically to unlock email- and flyer-to-calendar, though bundled with hardware they already bought, which raises lock-in and conversion. A phone-only app likely has to land at $5 to $10 a month or $40 to $80 a year.
- Cozi's Gold-over-Max skew (if accurate) suggests AI alone doesn't sell. Reliability of the core calendar and family sharing matters more.

### Gaps
- No current subscriber counts or paid-conversion rates for Cozi, Skylight, Ohai or Milo. No public revenue estimates (Sensor Tower/Appfigures) were retrieved. No RevenueCat "family" category data.

## 4. Segments that feel it most

### Takeaway
The strongest willingness to pay is among co-parents, who pay $77 to $350 per parent per year, often court-ordered. The largest volume segment is mothers in multi-kid, dual-working families with kids in club or travel sports.

### Cited Findings
- **Co-parenting apps are all paid in 2026.** AppClose reportedly ended its free tier Jan 1, 2026 (about $108/yr per parent; one source says $7.99/mo on web). OurFamilyWizard costs about $12.50 to $18 a month (about $138 to $216/yr per parent). TalkingParents removed its free tier in March 2026: Essentials $77/yr, Enhanced $177/yr, Ultimate $353/yr, each parent paying separately. Figures come from aggregator guides; verify on vendor sites. — [divorce.law (CA guide)](https://divorce.law/guides/co-parenting-apps-tools/california/); [TalkingParents vs OFW (vendor)](https://talkingparents.com/talkingparents-over-ofw); [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/co-parenting-apps-and-communication-tools-for-custody/)
- That co-parenting company OurFamilyWizard bought Cozi signals that incumbents see co-parents and general family organizing as one market. — [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/ourfamilywizard-acquires-cozi-1-family-organizing-app-301539136.html)
- **Sports families:** family spending on youth sports rose 46% over five years. Families spend about $1,016 a year on a child's primary sport, plus about $475 on secondary sports (about $1,500 per child in 2024). Travel is the largest cost line ($196 per sport per child in an earlier survey). Parents spend about 3h23m on each practice or game day. — [Project Play, Feb 2025](https://projectplay.org/news/2025/2/24/project-play-survey-family-spending-on-youth-sports-rises-46-over-five-years); [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/personal-finance/family-savings/what-families-are-really-spending-to-keep-kids-in-sports)
- **Mothers / default parent:** see section 1 (83% of mothers "mostly me" on the family calendar). — [JMF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jomf.13057)

### Inferences
- Sports parents already spend about $1.5K per child per year, so a $60/yr tool is noise in that budget. Sports schedules also change most often (weather, tournament brackets), which matches the "updates on change" feature.
- Co-parents pay, but they buy court-admissible records and messaging, not convenience, and incumbents are entrenched with courts. They are better treated as an adjacent segment than the wedge.

### Gaps
- No data found on the number of US co-parenting households, on dual-income share, or on the number of kids per family in relation to organizer-app adoption. No segment-level conversion data.

## 5. What parents say they want and complain about

### Takeaway
Complaints center on fragmentation (too many apps, passwords and emails), irrelevant noise, not knowing where information lives, and last-minute changes. Skylight owners complain about paywalled features. Direct Reddit evidence was not retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- Parents want one place for information. 19% "struggle to know where to find the information they need" and 24% get "too much irrelevant information" (McCrindle 2026, likely Australian). — [The Edu Flaneuse](https://theeduflaneuse.com/2026/08/23/the-cognitive-load-of-school-communication/)
- Mainstream framing: "Two kids, 12 apps, and zero clue" (Boston Globe, Oct 2026); "digital burnout" from classroom updates (Yahoo). — [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/05/lifestyle/parents-schools-edtech-apps-platforms-passwords/); [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/family-relationships/article/parents-signed-up-for-classroom-updates-they-got-digital-burnout-instead-090044802.html)
- Personal essay from a parent and writer: "Too Many Apps; Too Little Time." — [Ashlee Gadd, Substack](https://ashleegadd.substack.com/p/too-many-apps-too-little-time)
- Skylight owners resent that key features (AI import) sit behind a $79/yr subscription on top of the hardware. — [getsense.ai](https://getsense.ai/blog/posts/skylight-calendar-hidden-costs-paywall)
- Last-minute changes are the named frustration in multi-sport families. — [MHSAA](https://www.mhsaa.com/topics/viewpoints/parents-master-art-schedule-juggling?page=12)

### Inferences
- The promise to sell is "one place, only what matters to my kids, updated when it changes." Filtering out irrelevant information is as valuable as capturing it.

### Gaps
- Reddit (r/Parenting, r/Mommit, r/daddit) quotes were not retrievable via search snippets. Search returned no Reddit-quoting press. Worth a manual pass.

## 6. Channels and CAC signals

### Takeaway
No public CAC or LTV figures exist for Cozi, Skylight or similar family apps. Available benchmarks are generic, and the evidence favors trust-based channels (mom creators, word of mouth inside the household and school community).

### Cited Findings
- Agency benchmark for app CAC on Facebook: about $2.00 organic and $5.96 paid (secondhand, unaudited). — [Focus Digital](https://focus-digital.co/facebook-customer-acquisition-cost-by-industry-price-point/)
- Micro parenting creators (10K to 100K followers) charge about $150 to $1,500 per Instagram post and $300 to $3,000 per Reel. — [influencerfee.com](https://influencerfee.com/blog/parenting-influencer-cost/); [Sideshift](https://sideshift.app/blog/mom-influencer-marketing)
- Agencies argue mom creators work best where "trust and household adoption matter more than impulse reach." — [Archive](https://archive.com/blog/influencer-marketing-platforms-reach-moms)
- General app UA cost benchmarks: — [Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/marketplace/user-acquisition/research/user-acquisition-costs/)
- Competitors are investing in SEO comparison content ("Skylight hidden costs", "best AI family assistant 2026"). — [getsense.ai](https://getsense.ai/blog/posts/skylight-calendar-hidden-costs-paywall); [RemindHer](https://remindher.app/ai-family-assistant-app/)

### Inferences
- Built-in virality: every family calendar invites a second adult, sometimes grandparents or a nanny. Team or class-level sharing (one parent imports the team schedule, others subscribe) could act as a PTA- or team-level acquisition loop, but this is untested.

### Gaps
- No reported CAC or LTV for any family organizer app. No data on PTA or Facebook-group channel effectiveness.

## 7. iPhone share among US parents

### Takeaway
iPhone dominates among US teens (88%) and is the majority platform in the US overall, but I found no parent-specific iOS/Android split. A meaningful minority of households will include an Android adult, which matters for a shared calendar.

### Cited Findings
- Piper Sandler spring 2025 (n=6,455 teens, 43 states): 88% of US teens own an iPhone (85% a year earlier), and 88% intend an iPhone next. — [MacDailyNews](https://macdailynews.com/2025/04/09/88-of-american-teens-own-an-apple-iphone-piper-sandler/); [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/04/09/iphone-still-coveted-by-teens-but-other-apple-products-not-so-much)
- US smartphone OS share is about 58% iOS / 42% Android (secondary source; figures vary). — [Digital Silk](https://www.digitalsilk.com/digital-trends/iphone-vs-android-user-stats/)
- Asymco (Feb 2026) discusses teen iPhone users aging into adults and notes that parents likely buy teen iPhones (commentary, not survey). — [Asymco](https://asymco.com/2026/02/16/teen-survey-update-what-happens-when-teens-grow-up/)

### Inferences
- With roughly 40% of US smartphones on Android, an iPhone-only family app risks failing when one co-parent, a grandparent or a sitter is on Android. Subscribing to a calendar feed (ICS or Google Calendar) or a web view for non-iOS members would mitigate this without building an Android app.

### Gaps
- No survey of iOS vs Android among US parents of school-age kids, or of mixed-OS households, was found.
