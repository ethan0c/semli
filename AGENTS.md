# Semli

## Writing for people

Apply this voice when drafting or changing user-facing wording: app messages, website copy, screenshots, App Store text, emails, outreach, and social posts. Follow the existing requirements for the channel and locale.

- Write like one person explaining something to another. Use everyday words, contractions, and short paragraphs. Keep useful details; shorten wording that makes the reader work.
- Lead with what the reader can do or why you’re writing. Use a clear subject or title and a descriptive link or button. For outreach, ask for one useful response.
- Describe the actual action or result: “Check the dates before adding them” rather than “Experience seamless planning.” Keep error messages specific about what happened and what to try next.
- Keep the writer’s perspective consistent. Personal founder posts can use “I”; product messages can use “Semli” or “we.” Preserve the requested sender and sign-off instead of forcing every channel into the same voice.
- Avoid canned launch language, hype, jargon, defensive sales disclaimers, rhetorical question-and-answer hooks, and stock contrasts such as “The real value isn’t X, it’s Y.” Don’t add slang, fake anecdotes, or deliberate mistakes to sound human.
- State paid features and limits plainly beside the benefit. For example: “The calendar and reminders are free. Plus adds unlimited imports.”
- Preserve facts and meaningful qualifications. Never turn a small test set into a universal accuracy claim. Say “In those tests” when that is the evidence. Don’t invent user feedback, popularity, urgency, or personal experience.
- Check release status, feature availability, prices, and destination links against current sources before reusing an older draft. Dated beta plans are historical context, not proof of what is live today.
- Keep necessary consent, privacy, subscription, and permission details clear. Humanizing must not change what is promised or remove a restriction.

Before returning or saving a draft, make a quiet editing pass: would the sender actually say this, can the reader understand it on the first read, and are the claims supported? Rewrite stiff or vague lines before presenting the result. Apply this by default; the user should not need to ask for a separate “humanize” pass. Character counts and automated checks validate constraints, not whether the writing sounds natural.
