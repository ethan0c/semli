## Top complaints and praise in recent reviews (MyStudyLife, Structured, Tiimo, Shovel, Notion, Google Calendar)

### Takeaway
Across all the apps, the complaints are about money and reliability, not about missing features. Students object to useful basics moving behind a paywall, to trials that push them onto the most expensive plan, to sync and log-out bugs, and to redesigns that break their habits. The praise goes to a free tier that "doesn't pitch for money", to lifetime pricing, and to visual or ADHD-friendly daily planning. Google Calendar is still the default recommendation because it is free, works on every platform and is already the school's system.

### Cited Findings
- **MyStudyLife: paywall creep.** A 2025–26 analysis says that tagging task types (essay, group project, reading, meeting) now requires MyStudyLife+, at about $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year. Google Play reviewers say the free version has "only… a limited amount of tasks and items", and one says the redesign added "an expensive subscription fee that you have to sign up for." — [StudyToolGuide, "MyStudyLife in 2026: Red Flags"](https://studytoolguide.com/comparisons/is-mystudylife-still-worth-using-2026-red-flags-alternatives) (from a search summary); [AppBrain listing](https://www.appbrain.com/app/my-study-life-school-planner/com.virblue.mystudylife)
- **MyStudyLife: the redesign and its stability.** An App Store reviewer disliked the redesign. An August 2025 reviewer said the app "kept logging them out" and cancelled their subscription over it. Long-time users say recent updates made the app unstable. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/my-study-life-school-planner/id910639339); [MWM app page](https://mwm.ai/apps/my-study-life-school-planner/910639339)
- **MyStudyLife: what users praised before the redesign.** Users valued a free tier with no "pop-up pitches for money", and review sites describe the app as ad-free. Sources conflict on ads: one review says ads plus paid features make the new interface harder to use. — [SmartLearnAI review](https://smartlearnai.org/listing/mystudylife-review/); [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/my-study-life-school-planner/com.virblue.mystudylife)
- **Structured: AI output can't be edited before it is added.** An App Store reviewer said: "Once the list is created there is no option to edit what AI came up with without first adding the events to your day." — [Structured App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/structured-daily-planner-todo/id1499198946?see-all=reviews) (from a search summary)
- **Structured: sync and platform gaps.** iCloud sync is unreliable, moving tasks stutters and the app sometimes crashes. Android and web versions lag behind iOS, and the AI feature is Pro-only and missing from web. — [Saner.ai, Structured review 2026](https://blog.saner.ai/structured-review/); [ADHDProductivity review](https://adhdproductivity.org/structured-review-for-adhd/)
- **Structured: price.** A Google Play reviewer called $30 a year too high and asked for a cheaper lifetime option. The lifetime option is listed at $64.99 on the App Store and at $99.99 by one tracker; prices vary by region and date. Reviewers praise having a lifetime option at all as "a rare bit of user-friendliness." — [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/structured-daily-planner/io.unorderly.structured); [toolguide.io](https://toolguide.io/en/tool/structured/)
- **Tiimo: trial and cancellation complaints.** A Google Play reviewer called it "predatory" that the free trial is only offered on the most expensive plan. Another said their cancellation on the last trial day "did not process." In-app subscriptions get a 30-day trial, while the cheaper web subscriptions get only 7 days. Pricing in Dec 2025 was $12 a month or $54 a year. A 2026 guide says $7.99 a month or $79.99 a year. — [Tiimo on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tiimo.androidappreactnative&hl=en_US); [Daring Fireball, 2025 App Store Award winners](https://daringfireball.net/2025/12/2025_app_store_award_winners); [Lifestack, Tiimo pricing](https://lifestack.ai/blog/tiimo-pricing)
- **Tiimo: legitimacy and value.** Tiimo won a 2025 App Store Award, so Apple featuring it legitimises the visual or ADHD planner category. One aggregator reviewer said "with the existing bugs, I don't find it worth the subscription price." — [Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/2025/12/2025_app_store_award_winners); [JustUseApp Tiimo reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1480220328/tiimo-visual-daily-planner/reviews)
- **Shovel: setup is heavy and the price is the main complaint.** The App Store listing itself says "the fastest way for you to get started is to set everything up on your laptop in the Shovel web-app", which makes the phone app a companion. One reviewer said it is "harder to organize and plan your class schedule through the app than on their website" but "devs are super responsive." The price is the main complaint, at about $9.79 a month or $35 a year after a 7-day trial. One review cites "$449 to sign up then $198 yearly", which most likely refers to the bundled coaching course. Android ratings are 3.4 to 3.8. — [AppFollow, Shovel US](https://appfollow.io/ios/shovel-study-planner/1467742357?country=us); [Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/shovel-study-planner); [DormWay vs Shovel (vendor-written)](https://dormway.app/blog/dormway-vs-shovel)
- **Google Calendar is the default.** Guides call it the best calendar for most college students because it is free and cross-platform, and because "most colleges run on Google Workspace", which makes it "already the backbone of your academic life." — [VirtoSoftware, Best calendars for college 2026](https://www.virtosoftware.com/edu/best-calendars-for-college/); [Lifestack, best planner apps for college 2026](https://lifestack.ai/blog/best-planner-apps-for-college-students)
- **Paper vs app is framed as hybrid, not either-or.** Guides recommend "a digital calendar for appointments and deadlines, and a paper planner for daily tasks." Students with irregular schedules are said to benefit more from digital. — [Study Horizon, Paper planner vs app](https://www.study-horizon.com/productivity/planner-app-vs-paper); [Plum Paper](https://www.plumpaper.com/blog-digital-vs-paper-planning)
- **Notion:** I found no 2025–26 student review data on Notion specifically (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- **The free basics (differentiator 3) answer the most-cited complaint.** MyStudyLife's 2025 move of basics behind MyStudyLife+ appears to be the main source of recent negative reviews. A message like "the calendar, to-dos, reminders and repeating classes are free forever" speaks directly to that complaint. It also exposes Semli: if those basics are ever metered later, it will draw the same backlash.
- **The trial design matters as much as the price.** Tiimo's "predatory" complaint is about the trial structure, not the amount. If AI imports are metered, the meter should be transparent, with no auto-converting trials on the biggest plan.
- **The editable review step (differentiator 1) solves a real complaint.** The Structured reviewer couldn't edit AI output until it was already on the calendar. Semli's "review before anything is saved" design addresses exactly this.
- **iPhone-first is common.** Structured and Shovel both get criticised for weak non-iOS or mobile versions. That is evidence that platform gaps are noticed, but these apps still sell. The bigger risk for Semli is the missing web version for laptop-heavy setup (Shovel's "set up on your laptop").

### Gaps
- There are no verbatim Reddit quotes; reddit.com was not reachable from this session.
- I could not read App Store review pages directly, so review counts, star distributions and dates are unverified.
- I found no 2025–26 student-specific complaints about Notion as a planner. The common "too much setup / I spend more time building the system than using it" sentiment is folklore I could not source here.

## Do students trust AI to extract syllabus dates? Review step or full automation?

### Takeaway
Students use AI heavily, and most say AI output needs checking. Only a minority actually check consistently. The known ways syllabus parsing fails (invented times, wrong table rows, revision dates read as due dates, filled-in TBAs) are exactly the errors a source-linked review step catches. Universities are already telling students to check ChatGPT-built calendars against the syllabus or LMS. This supports differentiator 1 as both a trust feature and a safety feature. I found no student voice asking for fully hands-off automation.

### Cited Findings
- **Most students say verification is necessary.** In the CSU system survey (fall 2025, 94,060 respondents), 88% of students said verifying AI-generated content is necessary, against 97% of faculty. — [CSU "Ahead of the Curve" summary](https://www.calstate.edu/impact-of-the-csu/technology/ai-empowered-csu/Documents/csu-ahead-of-the-curve-summary.pdf?v=2)
- **Duke students expect inaccuracy.** 75% believe AI gives inaccurate answers, and 90% want AI to be transparent about its limitations. — [Duke CTL AI student survey](https://ctl.duke.edu/caradite/ai-student-survey/ai-accuracy-and-limitations/)
- **Stated trust and actual behaviour diverge.** In an AERA 2026 paper with 1,289 students, 40% were "Uncritical" (high trust, low verification), 38% "Inconsistent" and only 22% "Critical" (low trust, high verification). — [AERA 2026 paper](https://convention2.allacademic.com/one/aera/aera26/online_program_direct_link/view_paper/2272031/)
- **AI use is near-universal.** 85% of students used AI for coursework in the past year (Inside Higher Ed Student Voice 2025, via eWeek). — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/student-voice-survey-2025/)
- **A university tells students to check AI calendars.** Farmingdale State (Sept 2025) teaches students to build a semester calendar with ChatGPT but to "verify dates/times against the official syllabus or Brightspace before importing… If they conflict, Brightspace wins." — [Farmingdale State College](https://www.farmingdale.edu/news/ram-program/2025/2025-09-04-build-your-semester-calendar-with-chatgpt-google-calendar.shtml)
- **Known failure modes (vendor or guide sources, descriptive only):**
  - invented times: "a fake 9am slot looks authoritative and is exactly the kind of quiet error that makes you show up at the wrong hour"
  - table columns read in the wrong order
  - revision or publication dates mistaken for deadlines
  - AI filling in TBA dates
  - general-purpose AI "confidently inventing" a due date
  - Mitigations cited: source-linked entries back to the syllabus page, and creating events only for items the syllabus actually dates.
  — [scholarly.so](https://scholarly.so/blog/how-to-turn-a-syllabus-into-a-calendar-with-ai); [Qumely, AI syllabus parser guide](https://qumely.com/blog/ai-syllabus-parser-guide); [DormWay, AI syllabus parser 2026](https://dormway.app/blog/ai-syllabus-parser-automating-your-academic-schedule-in-2026); [Wandering Educators](https://www.wanderingeducators.com/education/tips/ai-hallucinations-how-to-catch-what-ai-makes-up)
- **OpenAI's College Planner "can get dates wrong."** A planner that remembers your list "can carry one wrong date forward for months." This is about admissions deadlines, but the risk is the same. — [ConnectPrep, 2026](https://www.connectprep.com/chatgpt-college-planner/)
- **Syllabus formatting itself is a problem.** MIT Sloan argues that if AI can't parse a syllabus, students can't either, so poorly structured syllabi are common. — [MIT Sloan Teaching & Learning Technologies, Feb 2025](https://mitsloanedtech.mit.edu/2025/02/27/if-ai-cant-read-your-syllabus-neither-can-your-students/)
- **Competitors already ship syllabus import.** Shovel supports PDF syllabus upload and imports from Canvas, Brightspace, Moodle and Google Classroom. DormWay and Coursicle publish AI-syllabus content. — [Educational App Store, Shovel](https://www.educationalappstore.com/website/shovel-study-planner); [Coursicle blog](https://www.coursicle.com/blog/AISyllabus/)

### Inferences
- **The provenance view should be the headline, not "AI import."** Syllabus import alone is no longer unique (Shovel, DormWay, ChatGPT plus Google Calendar workflows). What sets Semli apart is showing where each date was read from and saving nothing until confirmed. This matches Duke students' 90% demand for transparency and the institutional "verify first" guidance.
- **Design the review step for the students who won't check.** Because about 40% of students over-trust AI, the review step should make checking fast: highlight low-confidence items, flag TBAs, and never invent times. Relying on students to read every row will not work for that group.
- **LMS import may matter more than syllabus PDFs.** Canvas and Brightspace are what universities treat as authoritative ("Brightspace wins"). Without LMS import, a student must keep two sources in sync. This is a competitive gap to weigh.

### Gaps
- I found no direct student posts (Reddit or TikTok) describing a specific app hallucinating a due date. The failure-mode evidence comes from vendor and guide blogs.
- I found no survey that asks students outright whether they prefer review-then-confirm or full automation.

## How much does setup time drive abandonment? Retention benchmarks

### Takeaway
Productivity apps keep about 5–10% of users at day 30 on iOS. That is better than the average app (about 4–5%) but still means 90% or more are gone within a month. I found no hard data isolating setup time as the cause. The circumstantial evidence points one way: competitors that need laptop setup get "harder on the app" complaints, and RevenueCat finds early engagement matters more than trial length.

### Cited Findings
- **Productivity day-30 retention is about 9–10%** in AppsFlyer-based tables. Other blogs give 10–18%, and one gives a 20% median under a different method. — [UXCam benchmarks](https://uxcam.com/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks/); [GetStream 2026 retention guide](https://getstream.io/blog/app-retention-guide/); [Fyresite](https://www.fyresite.com/mobile-app-retention-rate-benchmarks-by-category-day/)
- **The average iOS app keeps about 3.7–5.3% at day 30** (AppsFlyer, and Business of Apps citing AppsFlyer). Adjust 2024 data shows 8% on iOS against 6% on Android. — [UXCam](https://uxcam.com/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks/); [Apsteq 2026 benchmarks](https://apsteq.com/blog/app-retention-benchmarks/)
- **RevenueCat SOSA 2025:**
  - Productivity is among the categories with better conversion and retention because of habitual use.
  - Trials of 17–32 days convert best (42.5–45.7% trial-to-paid, depending on the metric), "but early engagement is critical — simply extending the trial period doesn't guarantee retention."
  - Hard paywalls convert about 5x better than freemium but carry higher churn risk.
  - Lower-priced apps retain better.
  - TechCrunch's write-up says even the top 5% of productivity apps make under $1,000 a month after a year. I could not confirm which edition that figure is from.
  — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025/); [Subscription Insider](https://subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-report-ais-dominance-retention-challenges-and-the-shift-away-from-pure-subscriptions); [SaaStr](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2678138)
- **Shovel's own listing pushes setup to the laptop.** One roundup says "setup requires a desktop or laptop since the mobile app is a companion." Import from Canvas, Brightspace, Moodle or Classroom "speeds initial setup." — [AppFollow Shovel](https://appfollow.io/ios/shovel-study-planner/1467742357?country=us); [Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/app/shovel-study-planner)

### Inferences
- **Fast setup (differentiator 4) is plausibly the main retention lever.** It is the "early engagement" RevenueCat points to. Because a semester is front-loaded (syllabus week), a planner that isn't set up in week 1 likely never gets set up. This is an inference, not measured.
- **Benchmarks for Semli:** at least 10% day-30 retention among installers would be strong for the category. Because planners are seasonal, measure retention from semester start, not by calendar month.

### Gaps
- I found no published data tying setup time or the number of onboarding steps to planner abandonment.
- I found no student-planner-specific retention numbers (MyStudyLife and Shovel don't publish them).
- Sources differ on how they define retention (classic vs rolling); the figures are not directly comparable.

## Do students pay for planners? Willingness to pay, subscription fatigue, free vs lifetime

### Takeaway
The evidence leans toward "students mostly won't pay for a planner subscription." The most-praised options are free tiers and lifetime purchases. Paywalls on basics and aggressive trials generate the most anger. Metering only the costly AI part matches what reviewers say is fair, though I found no direct survey of students on this.

### Cited Findings
- **Students would rather not use an app than pay.** Students "would rather not use the application / program than pay for it," and most of their favourite apps are free. This is a Polish university sample, not US, so treat it as directional only. — [Lupa & Wójcik, Silesian Univ. of Technology, 2024](https://managementpapers.polsl.pl/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/194-Lupa-W%C3%B3jcik.pdf)
- **Gen Z is cutting subscriptions.** 37% of Gen Z streaming users had cancelled at least one service because of subscription fatigue, and 29% more planned to. Secondary aggregators report this, and it is about streaming, not apps. — [Subscription Insider on Medium](https://medium.com/@subscriptioninsider/subscription-fatigue-is-real-heres-what-the-data-shows-9fa40d32087e); [TechRT](https://techrt.com/subscription-fatigue-statistics/)
- **Lifetime pricing is praised and requested.** Structured's lifetime option is praised as "rare user-friendliness," and a reviewer asked for a cheaper lifetime tier than $30 a year. — [AppBrain Structured](https://www.appbrain.com/app/structured-daily-planner/io.unorderly.structured); [toolguide.io](https://toolguide.io/en/tool/structured/)
- **Paywalls and trial design are the top complaints.** For MyStudyLife: basics moved behind a paywall, and "an expensive subscription fee." For Tiimo: "predatory" trial design. For Shovel: subscription price is "the main complaint in reviews." — sources as cited in section 1
- **Typical student-planner prices in 2025–26:**
  - MyStudyLife+ is about $29.99 a year.
  - Shovel is about $35 a year (one source says $9.79 a month).
  - Structured Pro is about $30 a year, or $64.99–99.99 lifetime.
  - Tiimo is $54–79.99 a year.
  — sources as cited above

### Inferences
- **The free basics (differentiator 3) are well aligned with student sentiment.** The proof point for students is never paywalling the calendar, reminders or repeating classes. A low-cost AI-import pack, or a lifetime or semester pass, is likely to land better than a monthly subscription. A "semester pass" ($X per term) fits student budgets and the academic calendar; this is untested.
- **Students get a free AI option elsewhere.** The real competitor for AI import is "ChatGPT (free) plus paste into Google Calendar", which Farmingdale literally teaches. Semli's paid value has to be the verification, provenance and one-tap save, not the extraction itself.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–26 US survey of college students' willingness to pay for planner or productivity apps.
- I found no data on what share of MyStudyLife or Shovel users pay.

## Is "Android-less" a real handicap among US college students?

### Takeaway
The cost is small at launch. Piper Sandler's teen survey (the cohort now entering college) shows 87–88% iPhone ownership. The bigger cost is the lack of a web or laptop version, and to a lesser degree the Android minority (about 12%) inside group or shared contexts.

### Cited Findings
- **Fall 2025 (50th survey):** 87% of US teens own an iPhone, and 17% expect to upgrade to an iPhone 17. — [Piper Sandler 50th survey](https://www.pipersandler.com/news/piper-sandler-completes-50th-semi-annual-teen-survey); [MacTech, Oct 2025](https://www.mactech.com/2025/10/09/survey-eighty-seven-percent-of-teens-report-they-own-an-iphone)
- **Spring 2025:** 88% iPhone ownership, from 6,455 teens. — [MacDailyNews](https://macdailynews.com/2025/04/09/88-of-american-teens-own-an-apple-iphone-piper-sandler); [Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/iphone-teens-survey-spring-2025); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/107496-teens-tech-almost-90-own-iphones-most-use.html)
- **No current college-specific split.** I found no 2025–26 survey of iPhone vs Android among college students. The only college-level data is a 2013 eMarketer/Statista figure (38% iPhone vs 29% Android among degree holders), which is obsolete. — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/271229/anroid-vs-iphone-mobile-owners-education-level-us)
- **Google Calendar's cross-platform reach is a selling point** in student guides. — [VirtoSoftware](https://www.virtosoftware.com/edu/best-calendars-for-college/)
- **Competitors are criticised for weak web versions.** Structured's web and Android versions lag and lack AI. Shovel's mobile app is weaker than its web version. — [Saner.ai](https://blog.saner.ai/structured-review/); [AppFollow Shovel](https://appfollow.io/ios/shovel-study-planner/1467742357?country=us)

### Inferences
- **"iPhone only" (differentiator 6) is not a real handicap for individual US students.** It matters more for:
  - (a) community college and lower-income segments, where Android share is likely higher (this is unsourced and needs checking)
  - (b) any future sharing or study-group features
  - (c) syllabus PDFs, which live on laptops; that makes the coming web planner (or at least web or desktop upload) more important than Android.
- **Teen ownership is a proxy.** College students are 18–24, and Piper Sandler surveys high schoolers, so the share may be slightly lower in college.

### Gaps
- There is no 2025–26 measurement of iPhone share among US college students. Piper Sandler's teen figure is the best proxy.

## What would students switch for? Plus the case for shifts, classes and deadlines in one plan

### Takeaway
Going by guides and reviews (not verified Reddit), students switch away from a planner for four reasons: (a) price or a paywall change, (b) bugs, sync or log-outs, (c) a redesign that breaks their habits, and (d) setup friction. They switch toward an app for free basics, fast import from where their deadlines already live (syllabus, LMS), visual daily planning and reliability. Working students are numerous, but I found no student voice specifically asking for "shifts + classes" planning. That differentiator is plausible but unproven.

### Cited Findings
- **Reasons students switch away:** MyStudyLife's 2025 redesign plus paywall drove cancellations ("kept logging them out", "expensive subscription fee"). Structured's sync bugs and Tiimo's trial and cancellation issues are cited as reasons to look elsewhere. — sources in section 1
- **A hybrid paper and digital setup is the common recommendation**, so a planner app competes with paper for daily tasks and wins mainly on dated deadlines. — [Study Horizon](https://www.study-horizon.com/productivity/planner-app-vs-paper); [Plum Paper](https://www.plumpaper.com/blog-digital-vs-paper-planning)
- **Working students are numerous, but the figures are dated:**
  - NCES 2019–20: 43% of full-time undergraduates worked while enrolled.
  - Older NCES analyses: about 40% of full-time and 74% of part-time undergraduates worked.
  - Georgetown CEW: "Seventy percent of college students work while enrolled." The underlying data year was not visible.
  — [Gitnux aggregator, citing NCES](https://gitnux.org/working-college-students-statistics/); [Georgetown CEW](https://cew.georgetown.edu/corporate-2-news/page/211/); [Chronicle](https://www.chronicle.com/article/most-students-take-off-campus-jobs-report-says/)
- **None of the planner apps reviewed is pitched around shifts plus classes.** MyStudyLife, Shovel, Structured and Tiimo are built around classes, tasks or study time. That is my reading of the product descriptions in the sources above, not a claim from any source.

### Inferences
- **Shifts plus classes in one plan (differentiator 2)** reaches a large group: roughly 40–70% of undergraduates work, depending on source and definition. Competitors don't target it, so it is a credible gap. But there is no evidence here that students search for it or would switch for it; it needs direct validation (interviews, Reddit by hand). Students who work hourly retail or food-service jobs with changing schedules would gain the most from overlap warnings.
- **Local-first privacy (differentiator 5)** never came up as a student concern in any source found here. Student complaints are about price and reliability, not data. It may matter as a trust signal alongside AI import ("your syllabus isn't used for training"), but it is unlikely to drive adoption alone.
- **Evidence by differentiator:**

  | # | Differentiator | Evidence |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | AI import with a review step | Strong support for the review step and provenance; import itself is becoming commodity |
  | 2 | Shifts, classes and deadlines | Plausible, unvalidated |
  | 3 | Free basics | Strongest support, directly counters MyStudyLife's backlash |
  | 4 | Fast setup | Supported indirectly by retention logic and Shovel's friction |
  | 5 | Local-first privacy | No student demand signal found |
  | 6 | iPhone only | Low cost (87–88% teen iPhone share); the missing web or laptop version is the bigger risk |

### Gaps
- There are no Reddit threads such as "best planner app for college" or "how do you organize syllabus deadlines" with real comments; reddit.com was unreachable from this session.
- I found no direct student statements asking for shift scheduling inside a planner.
- I found no student data on privacy concerns about planner or AI-import apps.
