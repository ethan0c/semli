## 1. Full list of worker-side shift/schedule apps: features, pricing, ratings

### Takeaway
The category is crowded and mature. At least six generic shift-calendar apps have 1M+ Android installs and 4.1 to 4.8 star ratings. Nearly all are freemium, with a small Pro upgrade (roughly $2 to $40) that unlocks calendar export, widgets and sync. A new wave of AI "photo of schedule to calendar" apps launched from late 2025 to 2026, all with negligible traction. None I found does schedule diffing (comparing a new schedule with the saved one), or flags conflicts across jobs from imported schedules.

### Inferences
- Six or more generic shift calendars with 1M+ installs means "shift calendar" keywords are contested by entrenched, high-rated incumbents with tens of thousands of reviews. A new app will not outrank them on generic terms quickly.
- Pro prices cluster low ($10/yr, $40 lifetime for Supershift; $35/yr for Nursly). This is the price anchor workers see.
- AI screenshot import is becoming common. App IDs above 6.75 billion indicate most of these apps launched in 2026. Screenshot reading alone is not a moat. The diff/merge step and cross-job conflict detection remain unclaimed in everything I found.
- Rotation apps (fire, police, nurse, plant workers with DuPont patterns) serve people with predictable cycles. Hourly retail and food-service workers get irregular schedules published weekly, which is the gap screenshot import addresses. Most incumbents are built around repeating patterns, not irregular weekly schedules.

## 2. NurseGrid: history, ownership, users, monetization

### Takeaway
NurseGrid is the clearest proof that a free, worker-side shift app can reach a large share of a profession. It was founded by a nurse, grew by word of mouth, and is now at about 670K MAU. It never became a standalone business, though. It was unprofitable when HealthStream bought it for about $25M in March 2020. Six years later, HealthStream is still testing ways to monetize the audience (education sales, job postings, loan partnerships), with tiny disclosed revenue (about $40K/month in education revenue as of Q3 2025).

### Cited Findings
- HealthStream acquired NurseGrid (Portland, OR) in March 2020 for about $25M in cash, including a roughly 10% stake it already held. It expected less than $0.5M of 2020 revenue from the deal, tied to NurseGrid Enterprise (a SaaS tool for managers). — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200309005857/en/HealthStream-Acquires-NurseGrid-1-Rated-Top-Downloaded); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2020/healthstream-pays-25m-acquire-portland-startup-nursegrid-maker-mobile-app-nurses/)
- At acquisition: over 260K MAU, over 40% of them active daily, 4.9 stars with 40K+ App Store reviews, about 20 employees, and not yet profitable. — [Training Industry](https://trainingindustry.com/press-release/compliance/healthstream-acquires-nursegrid-1-rated-and-top-downloaded-app-for-nurses/); [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2020/03/10/healthstream-acquires-nursegrid-mobile-nurse-app)
- Later filings report about 619K MAU ("approximately one in six US nurses", FY2024 10-K), then over 670K MAU and about 2,000 new nurses per week ("roughly one in five", Q4 2025 call, Feb 2026). — [10-K via CapEdge](https://capedge.com/filing/1095565/0001437749-25-005705/HSTM-10K-2024FY); [Pulse.bot Q4 2025 transcript](https://www.pulse.bot/healthcare/news/healthstream-inc-hstm-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript-6e3e1e00-50fe-4dfa-bd7c-21f037183543)
- Share-of-nurses claims conflict. The App Store listing says 1 in 8, the 10-K says 1 in 6, and the earnings call and website say 1 in 5. A tracker estimates about 7.1M lifetime iOS installs and about 384K iOS MAU (third-party estimate). — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id949731866); [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/949731866/trends)
- Monetization: HealthStream frames NurseGrid and its other "career networks" as a newer line for paid subscriptions and add-ons. Management described six early-stage strategies (direct education sales, job postings, loan consolidation partnerships, among others), with education revenue through NurseGrid of about $40K/month as of Q3 2025. In 2025 HealthStream also bought MissionCare Collective (myCNAjobs, about $40M) for a CNA and caregiver recruiting network. — [AOL/Q3 2025 transcript](https://www.aol.com/finance/healthstream-hstm-q3-2025-earnings-231916465.html); [Nashville Post](https://www.nashvillepost.com/business/healthstream-acquires-missioncare-collective-for-40m/article_f59fbdf1-ad5a-4196-8f79-21fc56969602.html)
- The third-party estimate of NurseGrid's 2021 revenue is $1.1M (Latka; low reliability). — [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/nursegrid)
- Growth was word of mouth: about 250 downloads in the first month, then "quickly shared with other nurses". Coworker schedule sharing is the built-in viral loop. — [NurseGrid blog](https://nursegrid.com/blog/how-to-make-a-nurse-app-that-nurses-want-to-use); [WSU Nursing](https://nursing.wsu.edu/2018/07/31/coug-nurse-channeled-schedule-problem-into-tech-startup-nursegrid/)
- NurseGrid added a personal calendar feature for work-life balance. — [NurseGrid news](https://nursegrid.com/news)

### Inferences
- NurseGrid's value has been as a distribution asset for an employer-side and B2B company (talent, education, recruiting), not as consumer subscription revenue. This is a strong signal that worker-side schedule apps monetize poorly on their own and get valued for audience.
- NurseGrid benefits from one profession with a professional identity (nurses), with coworkers on the same unit sharing schedules. Generic hourly workers in retail and food service lack that tight vertical network.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether NurseGrid has any consumer paid tier today. Searches did not establish that.
- No disclosed NurseGrid-specific revenue for 2025 or 2026.
- One transcript URL (fool.com) is dated May 2026 for Q4 2025, which is odd. The Feb 2026 date from other sources is more likely correct.

## 3. Employer-side apps' worker features and multi-employer aggregation

### Takeaway
Major employer-side scheduling tools give workers one-way iCal/WebCal feeds at best, refreshed every 2 to 24 hours, and some offer no external sync at all. Each feed covers one employer. I found no product that aggregates a worker's schedules across different employers' systems. Workers with two jobs must subscribe to several feeds by hand, or rely on a personal calendar app.

### Cited Findings
- 7shifts: personal calendar sync links that external calendars pull, refreshed every 2 to 12 hours depending on the calendar app. Google setup must happen on web or desktop, not mobile. Links cannot be revoked once shared. — [7shifts KB](https://kb.7shifts.com/hc/en-us/articles/4861793532691)
- Deputy: WebCal subscription link from the web app. Google may take 8 to 24 hours to update. A downloaded .ics is a one-time import. — [Deputy Help](https://help.deputy.com/hc/en-au/articles/4688796818703)
- When I Work and Sling: one-way iCal feeds to Google and Outlook. When I Work has a "Calendar Sync" menu. These are secondhand sources. — [Soon.works comparison](https://soon.works/compare/shift-schedulers-with-google-calendar-outlook-sync); [SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/resources/when-i-work-google-calendar-sync)
- Homebase: the worker app is free (clock in, schedules, messaging). A review lists "no sync with external calendar" as a drawback. — [Sonary review](https://sonary.com/reviews/homebase/homebase-scheduling/)
- Branch (originally shift-swapping, now worker payments) still lists schedules, swaps, coverage requests and reminders in its app, but within one employer. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/162029/Branch/)
- Market size for multi-jobholders: the BLS 2025 average was about 8.7 to 8.8M multiple jobholders (about 5.4% of employed), peaking at about 9.3M (5.7%) in Nov 2025. About 9.0M in Sept 2026 (secondary source citing BLS). 57.8% hold one full-time job plus part-time work. Women 5.9% vs men 4.9%. — [USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/answers/how-many-people-have-multiple-jobs/country/united-states/); [Statista](https://www.statista.com/chart/35609/number-of-americans-who-have-multiple-jobs); [Walnut Invest](https://walnutinvest.com/stats/side-hustle-statistics)

### Inferences
- Many hourly workers' schedules live inside apps with calendar feeds (7shifts, Deputy, When I Work). For those workers, a screenshot importer competes with a free, automatic, if slow and fiddly, feed. The strongest screenshot use cases are paper or breakroom schedules, UKG, Workday, HotSchedules or other portals without easy feeds, and group-chat schedule images. SnapShift's marketing explicitly targets "the printout on the breakroom wall".
- Because no product aggregates across employers, Semli's cross-job conflict flagging is a real gap. The core multi-jobholder audience is about 9M people, of whom only a subset are hourly with irregular schedules.

### Gaps
- I did not verify worker-side calendar export for UKG Ready/Dimensions, Workday mobile or HotSchedules (Fourth). Do not assume either way.
- I did not check whether any employer app (for example, Homebase or When I Work) lets one worker account link several unrelated employers.

## 4. Gig-work aggregators as precedent for paid worker-side tools

### Takeaway
Gig tools show that workers will pay about $6 to $15/month only when the tool plausibly makes or saves them money (earnings optimization, mileage tax deductions, gas discounts). Even then, pricing churns, platforms can cut off integrations, and paid subscriber counts are not disclosed.

### Cited Findings
- Gridwise Plus: a 2024 founders' announcement set the price at $9.99/mo or $72/yr ($6.99/mo grandfathered). A help article shows $14.99/mo or $107.99/yr (possibly outdated). A promo page shows "from $6/mo billed annually". Gridwise claims a network of 500K+ gig workers. No paid subscriber count found. — [Gridwise announcement](https://gridwise.io/announcements/details-on-new-plus-and-basic-plans/); [Gridwise help](https://help.gridwise.io/hc/en-us/articles/360061691773-How-Much-Does-Gridwise-Cost); [Gridwise Plus](https://gridwise.io/plus)
- Gridwise also sells data and services to gig platforms (a B2B line) and offers gas subsidies. — [Gridwise for gig platforms](https://gridwise.io/gridwise-for-gig-platforms); [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/startups/gridwise-gas-subsidies)
- Solo: $5.3M seed. Claims "1M+ gig workers" (marketing). Pricing is inconsistent: older tiers of $5.99, $11.99 and $19.99/mo; a 2026 guide says $14.99/mo; 7-day trial. — [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/solo-raises-5m-gig-workers-platform); [Rideshare Guy](https://therideshareguy.com/solo-app-review/); [Solo llms.txt](https://www.worksolo.com/llms.txt)
- Para: DoorDash tightened login security and cut off Para's access. An app-store listing said Para would shut down May 1, 2024, yet CB Insights lists a $4.75M Series A-II in May 2025. These conflict, so Para's status is unresolved. — [SplitMetrics](https://splitmetrics.com/apps/para-gig-drivers-earn-more/id1548322258); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/withpara/financials)

### Inferences
- A schedule app's value is mostly about avoiding loss (missed shift, double-booking), not earning more. That is a weaker willingness-to-pay story than "earn more per hour". Framing alarms and conflict detection as "never lose a shift or a paycheck" is the nearest analog.
- The Para episode shows a risk that applies to Semli: depending on scraping an employer system is fragile. Screenshot input sidesteps that.

### Gaps
- No verified paid-conversion or subscriber numbers for any of these apps.

## 5. Funding, acquisitions and shutdowns of worker-side scheduling startups, 2020 to 2026

### Takeaway
VC-backed worker-side scheduling plays have consistently either been absorbed by a larger company (NurseGrid to HealthStream, 2020) or pivoted to employer sales and financial products (Branch to earned wage access and payments; Shyft to an enterprise shift marketplace sold to retailers like Gap). I found no standalone consumer schedule app that raised notable venture funding from 2020 to 2026.

### Cited Findings
- NurseGrid: about $25M acquisition by HealthStream, March 2020 (see section 2). — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2020/healthstream-pays-25m-acquire-portland-startup-nursegrid-maker-mobile-app-nurses/)
- Branch (Minneapolis): launched around 2015 as a shift-swapping and scheduling app ($6.8M in 2017, then a $10M round led by March Capital). It added pay advances in 2019 and now positions itself as a workforce payments platform (earned wage access, instant pay). — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/enterprise/branch-messenger-helps-hourly-shift-workers-schedule-their-lives-on-mobile); [Training Industry](https://trainingindustry.com/press-release/performance-management/branch-messenger-closes-10-million-in-funding-led-by-march-capital-partners-to-fuel-growth/); [Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/minneapolis-startup-company-adds-pay-advances-to-scheduling-app-features/505900772/); [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/branch-expands-payroll-advance-app-to-all-hourly-workers)
- Shyft Technologies (Toronto, then Seattle): worker shift-swap marketplace. $1.5M in 2016, $6.5M Series A in Nov 2018 (Ignition, Madrona), $8M total. Sold enterprise-wide to Gap brands. No later funding or exit found. — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2018/shyft-lands-6-5m-help-retail-service-workers-easily-swap-schedules/); [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/shyft-raises-65m-to-fuel-workforce-management-app/542428/)
- Gig adjacent: Solo $5.3M seed; Para $4.75M Series A-II (May 2025, per CB Insights). — see section 4.

### Inferences
- The pattern is that worker-side audiences get monetized through employers, financial services (earned wage access, lending) or recruiting, not subscriptions. A consumer subscription app for hourly workers would be swimming against the precedent, though it would need far less capital than a VC-scale play.

### Gaps
- Most funding data here is pre-2020 or from aggregators. I found no 2023 to 2026 funding news for consumer shift-calendar apps. That likely means none exists, though index gaps are possible.
- I could not confirm Shyft's current status (acquired, operating or closed).

## 6. Channels: how these apps reach workers

### Takeaway
The evidence points to ASO plus organic, coworker-to-coworker sharing (NurseGrid's viral loop, Supershift calendar sharing) and institution-backed distribution (a credit union for FireSync, the IAFF Foundation for Pro-Calendar). I found no evidence of paid TikTok or creator campaigns by any shift-calendar app.

### Cited Findings
- NurseGrid grew by nurses sharing the app with each other. Its TikTok presence is a single organic post tagged #nursesoftiktok, with no evidence of a campaign. — [NurseGrid blog](https://nursegrid.com/blog/how-to-make-a-nurse-app-that-nurses-want-to-use)
- FireSync is distributed by Firefighters First Credit Union as a member tool, and the IAFF Foundation brands its own calendar: affinity and financial institutions as channel. — [Firefighters First CU](https://www.firefightersfirstcu.org/FireSync-App); [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/iaff-foundation-pro-calendar/412401290)
- Supershift and others market sharing a calendar with family and partners. My Shift Planner's PR angle is "Your app saved my marriage" (family coordination). — [Prolific North](https://www.prolificnorth.co.uk/?p=75646)
- Personal-finance apps such as Gerald publish "best shift work apps" listicles, which act as SEO and affiliate discovery. — [Gerald](https://joingerald.com/learn/work--income/best-shift-work-apps-for-iphone)
- Employer partnership is the route that scaled Shyft and Branch (sold to employers, with workers onboarded by them). — [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/shyft-raises-65m-to-fuel-workforce-management-app/542428/)

### Inferences
- **How hard is this market to reach?** It is moderately hard. Generic ASO terms are owned by 1M+ install incumbents, hourly workers are price-sensitive, and there is no single profession hub like nursing. The best unexploited hooks are specific pain moments, such as "my manager texted a new schedule photo" or "two jobs, one calendar", for which long-tail keywords like "schedule screenshot to calendar" and "two jobs schedule" are less contested. Other hooks are short-form video showing the photo-to-calendar moment and the change-detection moment, and family sharing (partner, co-parent, childcare) as the viral loop instead of coworkers.
- **How hard is it to monetize?** It is hard. Precedents point to (a) low one-time or about $10/yr Pro prices for calendars, (b) $6 to $15/mo only for earnings-raising gig tools, and (c) the large audience-building successes (NurseGrid, Branch) monetizing through B2B, recruiting or earned wage access rather than consumer subscriptions. A plausible consumer price for Semli's version is in the Supershift and Nursly band ($10 to $35/yr), with conversion likely low single digits. That is an inference; I found no conversion data.
- Differentiation still open as of Oct 2026, based on everything found: schedule diffing and merging, per-shift provenance ("where each was read"), cross-job, school and childcare conflict flags, and alarm-grade reminders tied to imported shifts.

### Gaps
- No data on CAC, paid UA spend or TikTok performance for any shift app.
- No Appfigures or Sensor Tower revenue estimates for any app (the sites could not be fetched).
