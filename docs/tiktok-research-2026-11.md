## What to do with the findings

Keep four posts a week. Make the screenshot joke understandable on its own, prepare four weeks at a time, and use Sunday to review and queue the next week. Test 11 am against 7 pm Eastern before choosing a permanent time. Keep current sounds and topical references out of the long-term stockpile.

The research supports these as experiments. It does **not** establish a winning schedule for a small college-planner account. Public examples give us creative ideas; Semli's own results must decide what stays.

## How to decide what stays

Record TikTok metrics at 48 hours and seven days. A Sunday pass can backfill older posts, but should record their actual observation age if it missed a checkpoint. The generated metrics file has empty fields for missing data; a blank is not zero.

- Primary creative metric: `100 × (saves + shares) / views`, per post, then take each cohort's median. Keep shares and saves separately too.
- Secondary: median views at the same observation age; comments; profile visits where available. For videos, average watch divided by duration and completion rate. Photo-post watch behavior is a separate comparison.
- Business outcome: weekly `?r=tiktok` bio clicks, followed by attributable installs/first imports where available. A platform-wide bio link does not identify which individual post caused a click. A spike after a post is a clue, not attribution.
- Choose a format to make more often only after at least four comparable posts in each group. A twofold save/share-rate advantage is a practical follow-up threshold from the existing plan, not statistical significance. If both groups have almost no saves/shares, there is no usable winner.
- Do not choose a time from one outlier. After eight mature posts per window, look for the same direction in both pillars and in median views, without worse save/share rates. If the result is mixed, keep rotating for another month. The threshold is operational judgment.
- If jokes get reactions but no profile visits or clicks, make one product-explaining post next: a real import with a short relatable hook. Re-check its claims and destination first. More memes alone do not resolve that problem.

## What a Sunday session should look like

The initial stockpile takes longer. Once four weeks exist, budget about 45–60 minutes each Sunday; this is a target to time, not a measured production speed.

1. **10 minutes: read results.** Log older posts and write one sentence about what the audience responded to. Note boosts, unusual dates and format changes.
2. **10 minutes: refresh trends.** Look at US, past seven days in Creative Center and a current editorial roundup. Find three recent examples in relevant student feeds, with dates and links. Keep a candidate only if it fits the audience and can be made from our own material.
3. **15 minutes: choose next week's four.** Review the screenshot and caption as a person. Drop weak jokes. Replenish four reserve drafts so the stockpile stays several weeks ahead.
4. **15–25 minutes: schedule and verify.** Upload the TikTok file, check caption/audio/disclosure, set the time, then confirm all four entries in the destination's scheduled queue. Record their IDs or permalinks. An exported calendar is not a scheduled post.

Long-horizon evergreen posts can be queued in advance; retain the Sunday review to catch expired references, release changes, and weak sequences. For topical content, keep the slot flexible until that Sunday's check. Use neutral licensed/original audio for truly unattended posting.

## Scheduling: what is actually available

The old “desktop only, about ten days” sentence is too definite. TikTok's [official scheduler announcement](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/introducing-video-scheduler-now-you-can-plan-tiktoks-in-advance) still says ten days; Buffer's [May 2026 guide](https://buffer.com/resources/how-to-schedule-tiktok-posts/) reports thirty days and Studio mobile scheduling. Verify the actual date picker, media type, and audio controls in **Semli's account**. If it offers only ten days, add the next seven days every Sunday and keep later weeks prepared locally.

The connected **Post Bridge** tools already offer future timestamps, TikTok photos, videos, and draft storage. There is no need to build a custom TikTok publishing API before trying that route. On Nov 1, its account inventory contained fitness profiles and **no Semli account**, so I did not upload this batch or create scheduled posts there. A supported field does not prove a specific media/account combination has published successfully.

When Semli is connected, validate one reviewed post in the scheduler, read its timestamp/media/account back, and confirm the first publication before loading the remainder. A Post Bridge draft stays in Post Bridge; it is different from its TikTok-specific draft option. The tools' TikTok music option can request automatic music, but it is not a way to choose or pre-clear a specific trending track.

TikTok requires the [commercial content disclosure setting](https://ads.us.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-the-content-disclosure-setting-for-creators) for content promoting a brand or product. Apply the own-brand setting for Semli promotion; a paid creator relationship needs its appropriate disclosure. Keep these publishing settings clear even when the creative is a joke.

## Next research pass: full account histories

The missing evidence is account-level cadence, especially small accounts, not another list of global “best times.” Each Sunday, audit two accounts from this panel: `@gizmo.ai`, StudyFetch creator accounts, `@structuredapp`, `@tiimoapp`, and two current small student/nursing creators found in the feed. Structured and Tiimo are watchlist candidates, not analyzed performance examples in this report.

Use the latest 12 non-pinned posts over at least 28 days, not just popular posts. Save URL, date/time as shown, timezone or “unknown,” format, duration, opening hook, visible metrics, capture date, sponsorship status or “unknown,” and product visibility. Include flops. Compute posts per week only if the history is complete; exclude pinned-order artifacts. Compare a candidate with that account's median at similar ages. Public likes/views cannot establish saves, watch time, paid distribution, conversions, or an optimal hour. Do not translate an unknown timezone into Eastern.

This produces a more useful comparison over time than copying a large ambassador network's daily output. Keep the 2025 cases as reference material and refresh the current examples weekly.
